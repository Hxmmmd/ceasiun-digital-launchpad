# Website Design & Build Brief — Premium Digital Agency Website

You are acting as a senior product designer, UI/UX designer, and frontend engineer.

Build a modern, premium, "techy" marketing website for a digital agency. Treat this

as a real client project — not a generic template.

## 1. Overall Vibe & Quality Bar

- The site should feel: modern, premium, techy, confident, professional, and

  digital-first — the kind of site a serious technology/digital agency would show

  off, not a generic template look.

- Visual and interaction quality must be AT LEAST as strong as a polished,

  dark-themed, animated SaaS/agency landing page — ideally better, never below

  that bar.

- Use tasteful motion: smooth scroll-triggered reveals, hover states, subtle

  parallax/depth where appropriate — but nothing gimmicky (avoid cheap 3D,

  overdone glassmorphism, or generic AI-template gradients).

- Include animated number/stat counters (e.g. years active, team size, projects

  delivered, clients served) that count up when scrolled into view.

- Confident typographic hierarchy, generous whitespace, consistent spacing rhythm.

- Fully responsive: desktop, laptop/tablet, and mobile must each be deliberately

  designed, not just auto-shrunk. Mobile needs its own nav pattern (hamburger

  menu), stacked layouts, and touch-friendly spacing/tap targets.

## 2. Sitemap & Page-by-Page Requirements

### Home

- Navbar (logo, nav links, primary CTA button)

- Hero section (headline, subheadline, CTA, supporting visual)

- Services overview (summary cards for the 9 service categories, linking to

  the Services page)

- Why-choose-us / differentiators section

- Animated stats/numbers section

- Testimonials section

- FAQ section

- Contact/CTA section

- Footer (with social links)

### Services

- Page intro/header

- Full breakdown of each of the 9 service categories and their sub-services

  (cards or tabbed/accordion sections — see BUSINESS CONTEXT)

- Cross-links to relevant case studies

- Footer

### Work / Portfolio

- Page intro

- Project/case-study grid (filterable by category if feasible)

- Individual case-study template (problem → approach → result)

- Footer

### Process

- Page intro

- Step-by-step process/methodology timeline using the 8-step process defined

  in BUSINESS CONTEXT (Discovery → ... → Continuous Improvement)

- Optionally illustrate the two engagement workflows (project-based vs.

  monthly retainer)

- Contact CTA

- Footer

### About

- Page intro

- Company story section (founding year, founder, growth from 10 to 21

  team members — factual, no invented drama)

- Team/culture section (no invented individual names, photos, or awards

  beyond what's given — placeholder-ready structure only)

- CTA

- Footer

### Careers

- Page intro

- Culture/why-work-here section

- Open-roles list (placeholder-friendly structure)

- CTA

- Footer

### Contact

- Page intro

- Contact form (name, email, phone, message, service interested in)

- Direct contact details (WhatsApp/phone, social links) from BUSINESS CONTEXT

- FAQ section

- Footer

### Blog (new)

- Blog listing page — grid/list of posts, category filter, search

- Individual blog post template — title, cover image, author, date, rich

  content body, related posts

### Admin / CMS Panel (new)

- Simple, login-protected admin area (authentication required)

- Create / edit / delete blog posts: title, cover image, category or tags,

  rich-text or markdown body, draft/publish toggle

- Basic media upload for post images

- Single dashboard to view and manage all existing posts

## 3. Improvements Over a Generic Template

- Strengthen visual hierarchy, spacing, and CTA placement wherever a plain

  template would feel flat.

- Avoid: childish visuals, excessive gradients/glassmorphism, unnecessary 3D

  decoration, generic AI-generated-looking layouts, exaggerated claims, cheap

  freelancer-style presentation.

- Every section should feel intentional and brand-specific — not a copy-pasted

  stock layout.

## 4. Branding & Color Palette — IMPORTANT

- Do NOT invent or default to your own color palette.

- Use ONLY the colors, logo, and brand details provided in the "BUSINESS

  CONTEXT" section below.

- No specific brand colors are given yet — until they're specified, use a

  minimal, premium neutral dark palette (near-black background, off-white

  text, one restrained accent color) rather than inventing a full palette.

## 5. Technical Notes

- Fully responsive across desktop, laptop, tablet, and mobile.

- Clean, semantic structure so pages are easy to extend later (new blog posts,

  new case studies, new services) without a full redesign.
